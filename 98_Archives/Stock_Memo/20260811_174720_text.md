Company Briefing summary

2Q margin expansion: Gross margin increased by 9ppt QoQ to GM 24.75%, OPM 21%, with roughly half driven by price hikes and half by product mix. Utilization was broadly unchanged QoQ except for PCB.
Revenue mix: ABF 55–60%, BT 30–35%, and PCB 10–15%.
Utilization in 2Q: ABF was around 80%, BT was fully loaded, and PCB was 70–75%.
Product margins in 2Q: ABF GM improved to low 30% from low 20%; BT gross margins improved to low-20% range from high single digit%.  PCB continued to record a mid-to-high single-digit gross loss. BT margin remains roughly 10ppt below ABF.
3Q guidance: Revenue is expected to grow at least high teens QoQ, potentially exceeding 20%, driven by high-end switches and additional ASIC projects. Gross margin should improve further, although the Kunshan ramp may create near-term learning-curve pressure.
Kunshan ramp: Next-generation ABF substrates for 1.6T switches will enter mass production in 3Q. Utilization is expected to reach 100%, supporting rapid margin improvement as bottlenecks are removed.
ABF outlook: Management expects an ABF capacity shortage in 2H26, with demand supported by CSP capex and new ASIC projects. Order visibility exceeds one year, and tight lead times could persist through 2027–28.
BT outlook: Pricing and supply-demand conditions are even stronger, as several suppliers have exited after nearly a decade without meaningful capacity additions. BT has seen quarterly price increases of approximately 20–30%, mainly reflecting higher costs.
T-glass constraint: T-glass remains the primary cost and pricing driver. Major new supply is unlikely before 2027, while next-generation ABF designs may increase usage from one layer currently to 3–4 layers, potentially extending tightness into 2028 or beyond.
Pricing: Nanya PCB is passing higher material costs through to customers. ABF pricing is negotiated order by order, while BT pricing hike is higher than ABF price hike in 2Q, and co expecting another 20-30% QoQ price increase in Q3
Capacity expansion: Chiayi will be the main expansion site over the next 3–5 years, focused on high-end substrates and multilayer PCBs. Equipment is also constrained, with most lead times close to or above one year.
Funding options: The company is considering internal funds, bank loans, customer prepayments and LTAs. However, LTAs could reduce future pricing flexibility.
High-end substrate ASP: Management said next-generation advanced packaging substrate ASPs above US$1,000 are reasonable, with some products reaching US$1,500+.
Server CPU opportunity: CPU substrate contribution is limited this year due to capacity constraints but should increase in 2027, leveraging relationships with existing GPU/graphics customers

ABF: approximately 80%

Q: What are the gross margins by product in 2Q?
ABF: low-30% range
BT: improved significantly to the low-20% range
PCB: still recording a mid-to-high single-digit gross loss
BT gross margin remains approximately 10ppt below ABF.
Q: What is the 3Q26 revenue outlook?
Management expects at least high-teens QoQ growth, with the possibility of exceeding 20% QoQ.
Growth will be driven mainly by ABF substrates for high-end switches and additional ASIC projects.
BT is not expected to contribute meaningful volume growth because capacity is already fully utilized.
Q: What is the 3Q26 gross-margin outlook?
Gross margin should improve further from 2Q26.
Market expectations of overall gross margin exceeding 30% may be achievable, but management would not confirm this.
The Kunshan plant is ramping next-generation products, so learning-curve costs may temporarily dilute margins.
Q: What is the outlook for the Kunshan plant?
Next-generation ABF substrates for 1.6T switches will enter mass production in 3Q26.
Utilization is expected to reach 100%.
Management expects margins to improve rapidly after eliminating production bottlenecks.
The longer-term target is for Kunshan’s profitability to approach that of the Taiwan operations.
Q: How does BT pricing compare with ABF pricing?
BT pricing has been stronger and more consistent than ABF.
BT price increases have been around 20–30% QoQ, mainly reflecting higher raw-material costs.
ABF pricing is more complicated because it is negotiated order by order.
A consolidated ABF pricing trend will only become clear near the end of each quarter.
Q: Why is BT supply becoming tight?
There has been almost no meaningful BT capacity expansion over the past decade.
Several suppliers have gradually exited or reduced BT production after years of severe oversupply.
With the memory industry entering an upcycle, demand is recovering against a structurally reduced supply base.
Management believes BT supply-demand conditions should continue to tighten.
Q: Could Nanya PCB expand BT capacity?
Management remains open-minded but has not committed to expansion.
If BT gross margin can improve toward 40–50%, additional capacity could be considered.
Current BT capacity is already fully loaded.
Q: What is the ABF supply-demand outlook?
Management expects ABF capacity to become insufficient in 2H26.
Demand is structurally supported by CSP capex, ASIC projects, high-end switches and other AI applications.
Customers are placing orders earlier because they expect future capacity shortages.
Order visibility already exceeds one year.
Tight lead times could persist through 2027 and possibly 2028.
Q: Is there an oversupply risk as competitors expand capacity?
Management said it is difficult to quantify industry-wide supply and demand.
Expansion is constrained by long equipment lead times and shortages of key materials.
The company sees the risk as lower for BT because industry capacity has barely increased and some supply has exited.
For ABF, strong structural AI demand should help absorb new capacity.
Q: What is driving substrate price increases?
The main driver is rising raw-material costs, particularly the shortage of T-glass.
Nanya PCB has not seen any sign of easing in T-glass supply.
The company intends to pass higher costs through to customers and secure reasonable pricing.
Q: When could T-glass supply improve?
Nittobo remains the major supplier.
Meaningful new capacity is not expected to come online until 2027.
Management expects supply conditions to become tighter before the new capacity arrives.
Q: Why could T-glass tightness extend beyond 2027?
If move from 1 core to 3-4 cores, the T-glass usage will increase 3-4 times
Higher consumption per substrate could offset new supply additions.
Management therefore believes tightness could extend into 2028 or longer.
Q: Is the company developing alternatives to pure T-glass solutions?
Yes. Nanya PCB is developing and qualifying hybrid solutions that combine E-glass and T-glass.
The actual number and combination of layers will depend on each customer’s product design.
Q: What is the company’s capacity-expansion plan?
Chiayi will be its primary expansion site over the next 3–5 years.
New capacity will focus on high-end substrates and multilayer PCBs.
Management did not disclose detailed capacity figures or the exact product allocation.
Q: What are the main constraints on capacity expansion?
Equipment availability is a major bottleneck.
Lead times for almost all key equipment are close to or above one year.
Expansion therefore cannot be completed quickly even if customer demand is strong.
Q: How will the expansion be funded?
Options include internal cash, bank loans, customer prepayments and customer consignment arrangements.
Several customers are already discussing potential support.
No final decision has been made; any board-approved plan will be disclosed.
Q: Would the company sign long-term agreements with customers?
LTAs are one possible option.
However, management is cautious because an LTA may require the company to sacrifice future pricing flexibility.
Customer prepayment or consignment could also be considered for new capacity.
